A more personal introduction

Serious methods. Human partnership.

Behavioral Research and Teaching is a small University of Oregon research group in Eugene. We study student learning and academic assessment, develop tools for educators, and support the design and technical quality of state assessment systems.

Our work moves between scholarship and application. A research question may become a technical study, an assessment design, a clearer report, or a tool that helps an educator see what a student is ready to learn next.

We bring curiosity to the question, care to the collaboration, and precision to the evidence.

Interpreting growth through complementary evidence

Brock Rowley and Gerald Tindal analyze ORExt records from 2014–15 through 2025–26 using scale-score movement, achievement-level transitions, progress toward proficiency, reliable-growth classifications, and multi-year patterns.

Documented contribution: The study shows how converging indicators can strengthen growth interpretation for students with the most significant cognitive disabilities.

Testing the links from standards to items

Brock Rowley, Sevrina Tindal, Joseph Nese, and Aiden Bagshaw use five structured review phases, reviewer training, and defined rubrics to examine standards, alternate achievement expectations, complexity, accessibility, and field-test items.

Documented outcome: Most reviewed standards were strongly linked, item reviews showed strong or sufficient alignment, and the study identified actionable areas for continuous improvement.

A national view of alternate assessment

Brock Rowley, Gerald Tindal, and Dan Farley synthesize state repositories, federal reviews, and national evidence across participation, accessibility, accommodations, technical analysis, accountability, and reporting.

Meaningful use: The report provides a common evidence base for comparing state practice and identifying questions for a national assessment center.

Researcher-led specialty

Assessment Forensics & Test Security

Independent analysis of response patterns, response times, and administration evidence to help assessment programs identify unusual results for structured investigation.

Explore assessment forensics

Start with the decision

Define the security question, available data, comparison groups, thresholds, limitations, and investigation protocol.

Examine multiple signals

Evaluate response-time and response-pattern evidence, person fit, response similarity, model performance, and false-positive risk.

Deliver evidence people can use

Provide reproducible analysis, documented uncertainty, alternative explanations, and recommendations for further review.

Research connection

Cengiz Zopluoglu’s assessment-forensics work

Specific research includes simultaneous detection of compromised items and examinees with item preknowledge, response-time modeling, and machine-learning approaches to detection.
