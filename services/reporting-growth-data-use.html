Technical service 04

Reporting, Growth & Data Use

Make assessment results understandable and useful without exceeding what the evidence can support.

Discuss this serviceReview deliverables

A realistic state challenge

A new score report or growth measure must serve multiple audiences, but the state is uncertain which interpretations are defensible.

Leaders need to reconcile technical properties, longitudinal data, policy goals, subgroup considerations, and the actions users may take from reported results.

What BRT would investigate

  • Intended audiences, decisions, and interpretation claims
  • Properties and limitations of proposed growth measures
  • Clarity and accuracy of score displays and explanatory language
  • Longitudinal patterns, subgroup results, and data-use risks

Representative methods

Evaluate both measurement and communication

Growth and progress-monitoring evaluations compare complementary indicators rather than relying on one number alone.

  • Longitudinal and growth analysis
  • Reliable-change and achievement-transition analysis
  • Progress-monitoring model fit and decision-rule evaluation
  • Multi-year trajectory and sensitivity analysis
  • Score report content review
  • Interpretation claim mapping
  • Subgroup and sensitivity analysis
  • Stakeholder feedback protocols
  • Data-use scenario review

Specific deliverables

Growth study

Methods, results, subgroup considerations, limitations, and appropriate-use guidance.

Reporting review

Findings on displays, terminology, explanatory content, accessibility, and interpretation risk.

Interpretation guide

Audience-centered explanations, examples, cautions, and recommended data-use practices.

Featured BRT publication

Interpreting Longitudinal Growth in Alternate Assessments Through Complementary Evidence (2026)

Brock Rowley and Gerald Tindal

This study analyzes ORExt Grades 3-8 English Language Arts and mathematics from 2014-15 through 2025-26. It integrates complementary indicators of growth, including scale-score movement, reliable change, achievement-level transitions, progress toward proficiency, and multi-year patterns.
